### What is photosynthesis?
Photosynthesis is a process by which plants, algae, and certain bacteria convert light energy from the sun into chemical energy stored in glucose. This process is essential for life on Earth, as it provides the primary energy source for nearly all living organisms.

Photosynthesis is a complex process that involves two main components: light energy and carbon dioxide (CO2). During this process, plants, algae, and certain bacteria convert sunlight into chemical energy.

The process begins when sunlight is absorbed by the leaves of plants, and then it is used to generate energy in the form of ATP (adenosine triphosphate) and NADPH (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate). The energy from these chemical reactions is used to convert CO2 into glucose.

The glucose produced is used by the plant to grow, and the energy is released as ATP. The glucose is then used by the plant to grow and mature, and the glucose is stored in the form of starch.
